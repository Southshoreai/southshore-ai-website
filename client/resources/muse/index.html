Start here

Muse is not just another chatbot.

Most AI tools wait for a question, write an answer, and stop. Muse is designed to help carry work forward. Explain the outcome you want, share the important details, set a boundary, and ask it to return with options for your approval.

Muse can help with reminders, planning, research, documents, files, and selected connected services. It can also keep context within a conversation, so you do not need to start from zero each time.

The useful mindset: Do not ask, “What can AI do?” Ask, “What is making my week heavier than it needs to be?”

The one rule that makes Muse better

Give Muse four things: the outcome, the context, the boundary, and the finish line.

“Help me plan a calm week. Look at my calendar, identify the three most important commitments, and give me a one-page plan. Do not change anything on my calendar. Ask before sending or booking anything.”

Five everyday ways to make room

Give the busywork a better place to go.

01 / THE SUNDAY RESETPlan first

Turn the weekly pile into a plan you can actually follow.

You have a calendar, a half-written grocery list, notes in three places, and the sense that something is missing. Muse can turn the scattered pile into a calm plan.

“I want a calmer week. I have work meetings, two family commitments, and a list of errands. Help me make a realistic Monday-to-Sunday plan with no more than three priorities per day. Include a grocery list and a 30-minute Sunday prep list. Do not add anything to my calendar yet.”
  • Tell Muse your work hours, energy limits, and non-negotiables.
  • Keep chores short: “Make each one under 25 minutes.”
  • Let yourself decide what deserves a calendar block—or no action at all.
02 / THE COMPLICATED ERRANDReview changes

Give the research, comparisons, and waiting time to someone else.

A return, airline question, contractor search, or forgotten subscription can consume an entire evening. Muse can gather facts, organize options, and prepare the next step.

“I need to deal with my [company or issue]. Find the relevant policy, summarize my options in plain English, and prepare the information I need for a call or request. Do not submit, cancel, buy, or change anything without showing me the final step first.”
  • Provide order numbers, dates, or the relevant email thread.
  • Set a boundary: “Only search this one email thread.”
  • Let Muse do the homework; you make the decision.
03 / THE MOVE, TRIP, OR BIG EVENTChoose before booking

Replace dozens of tabs with one clear decision trail.

Big plans create dozens of small decisions. Muse can break the project into steps, compare options, and keep the details together.

“I am planning [a move / a trip / a birthday gathering] for [date]. My budget is [amount]. My priorities are [three priorities]. Give me a step-by-step plan, a decision checklist, and the top three options for anything I need to book. Show me the trade-offs. Let me choose before you book or message anyone.”
  • Ask for a packing list, agenda, or shared shopping plan.
  • Flag deadlines before they become urgent.
  • Check live availability, addresses, total costs, and cancellation terms yourself.
04 / THE SOLO PROFESSIONAL’S WEEKDraft, do not send

Turn loose notes into a tighter work rhythm.

Freelancers, consultants, creators, and job seekers often do the work of a small team. Muse can help turn a fuzzy week into visible progress.

“I run my own work. Review the priorities I share below and help me plan a focused week. Create a client follow-up list, a draft project update, and a simple Friday review. Keep my tone warm and direct. Do not send any messages without my approval.”
  • Upload a brief or rough outline and ask for a cleaner draft.
  • Ask for three versions: short, warm, and executive.
  • Read drafts for names, numbers, promises, and tone before they reach anyone else.
05 / THE SMALL-BUSINESS MONDAYAccess is a choice

Find the next useful move before the inbox takes over.

You probably do not need more ideas. You need a sharper view of what matters, what is slipping, and what should happen next.

“Help me start the week as a small-business owner. Based on the business information and tools I choose to connect, identify the top five things that need attention. Separate urgent issues from good opportunities. Draft a simple one-week action plan. Do not publish, send, spend, or change settings without asking me.”
  • Start with only the tools that matter for this task.
  • Ask Muse to explain what it sees before you ask it to act.
  • Keep a human accountant or advisor involved where a decision requires one.
Copy, then make it yours

Seven prompts worth keeping.

The inbox reset

“Summarize the emails I mark as important. Group them into: I need to respond, I need to decide, and I can ignore. Draft replies, but do not send them.”

The decision brief

“I need to choose between [option A], [option B], and [option C]. Build a comparison table based on cost, time, risk, and what I care about most: [priorities]. Tell me what information is missing.”

The weekly meal plan

“Plan five easy dinners for [number] people. Consider [dietary needs], a budget of [amount], and dinners that take under [time]. Make one grocery list grouped by store section.”

The follow-up writer

“Turn these notes into a clear follow-up email. Keep it under 150 words, make the next step unmistakable, and show me the draft before it is sent.”

The meeting rescue

“I have a meeting about [topic] on [date]. Create a short agenda, the five questions I should answer beforehand, and a follow-up checklist. Do not email anyone.”

The life-admin list

“Help me sort this list into: do now, schedule, delegate, and let go. Ask one question when you need more context. Keep the result to one screen.”

The goal check-in

“I want to make progress on [goal] over the next 30 days. Turn it into four weekly milestones and suggest the smallest action I can take today.”

The three approval rule

Keep your judgment where it matters.

Muse can be helpful because it can take real steps. That is also why your boundaries matter. Pause and review whenever an action could send, spend, or commit.

Send

Email, message, post, or share a file.

Spend

Purchase, book, subscribe, or change a price-sensitive plan.

Commit

Change a calendar, submit a form, accept terms, or speak for you.

For everything else, start small. Ask it to research, organize, draft, compare, plan, summarize, or remind. Trust grows from results you have actually reviewed.

When Muse misses the mark

Do not start over. Give a better instruction. Add an example when the output is too vague. Ask for five bullets when it is too long. Say “Do not take action; give me options only” when it is too bold. If something feels off, ask to see its assumptions or decision criteria.

Better prompting is not clever wording. It is clear context and a clear finish line.
